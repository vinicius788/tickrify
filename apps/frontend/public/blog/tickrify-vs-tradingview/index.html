What each one is actually for

TradingView is a charting and market-data platform. It's where you draw trendlines, set up indicators, build watchlists, get alerts, and — increasingly — talk to a built-in AI assistant about the chart you're looking at. It is broad, deep, and for most traders it's the home base. Nothing below is an argument to stop using it.

Tickrify does one narrow thing: you hand it a chart, it grounds the read in live candle data, and it commits to a structured verdict — direction, entry, stop, targets, a calibrated confidence tier — or it tells you WAIT, this setup isn't worth your money. That's the whole product. It's a checkpoint, not a workspace.

Where they overlap — and where they don't

The overlap is TradingView's AI assistant. If you're happy asking the in-chart copilot "what do you think of this setup?" and you live inside TradingView anyway, you may not need a second tool — and we'd rather you know that than pay us out of confusion. Two honest differences, though: TradingView's assistant is built to be helpful about the chart in front of it, which means it rarely tells you there's no trade here. And its confidence, when it offers any, isn't tied to a measured track record of its own past reads. Tickrify is built around exactly those two things — a willingness to say WAIT, and a confidence tier that's calibrated against outcomes we track, wins and losses alike.
