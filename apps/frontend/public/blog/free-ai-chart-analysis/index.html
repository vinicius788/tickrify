The four kinds of free

"Free" in this category means four different things, and they are not interchangeable.

If a page says "free" and asks for a card before the first analysis, you are in row four. That is a legitimate business model, but it should not appear in a list titled "free".

What a general chat model does well

Better than most vendors will admit. Paste a chart screenshot into a current chat model with vision and ask for a structural read, and you will get a competent description of trend, obvious swing points, the range it is sitting in, and a plausible narrative. For learning to read a chart, it is remarkable, and it costs nothing.

Three things it does badly enough to cost you money:

  • Exact prices. It is reading an image. It will produce an entry at 1.0842 that looks precise and is wrong by enough to move your stop into noise. This is the single most expensive failure mode and it is invisible, because wrong numbers look identical to right ones.
  • Agreeableness. Ask "is this a good long?" and you will usually get reasons it might be. Chat models are tuned to be helpful, and "there is no trade here" does not feel helpful to a model optimising for your satisfaction.
  • Consistency. The same chart on Tuesday and Thursday can produce different reads, because nothing pins the process down. You cannot build a record out of an inconsistent reader.

The free prompt that closes most of the gap

Two of those three failures are fixable with wording. Paste this with your screenshot:

You are reviewing a chart I am considering trading. Rules:
1. Do not infer exact prices from the image. Ask me for the
   current price and the key levels, or state levels as
   structure ("above the prior swing high") rather than numbers.
2. You are allowed, and expected, to answer "no trade here".
   Most charts do not contain a setup.
3. Give me: market structure, the direction the structure
   favours, where the idea is invalidated, and the single
   strongest argument against the trade.
4. No encouragement. No "good luck". If the setup is marginal,
   say marginal.
Instrument: [X]  Timeframe: [Y]  My read: [one line]

That prompt, used every time, gets a free model most of the way to a usable second opinion. The longer version, plus the test that exposes the price problem in 30 seconds, is in our ChatGPT piece. If you will genuinely run it every time, including on the day you are down two trades and in a hurry, you do not need to buy anything.

What free tiers hide

Three things worth checking before you spend your free allowance:

  • Whether the tool is allowed to say no. A free tier exists to sell an upgrade, and a read that says "nothing here" does not sell upgrades. A tool that finds a setup on every chart you feed it, including a flat one, is filling a quota.
  • Whether anything grounds the read. Ask what data source produced the levels. "Our AI" is not an answer. Live candles, ATR and volume are answers, and the honest version admits where that grounding does not exist.
  • Whether results are published. Not testimonials or screenshots of wins. A page with a methodology, a sample size and the losses included. Most of this category has none, ourselves excluded, which is a low bar we cleared deliberately.
The fastest test of any free chart tool: send it a featureless, flat chart. What it says next tells you what it is for.

Three jobs where free is enough

We would rather you keep your money than churn in a month. Free is the right answer if your job is:

  1. Learning to read structure. A chat model explaining why a break of structure is a break of structure, on chart after chart, is a genuinely good teacher and there is no reason to pay for that.
  2. Occasional sanity checks. A few charts a month, no routine to maintain. A careful prompt covers it.
  3. Arithmetic. Position size, risk-reward and the win rate a given ratio requires are calculations, not analysis. Ours are free and require no account at tickrify.com/tools, and they run entirely in your browser.

Four places free genuinely runs out

And the honest case for the other side:

  • Precise levels. If you need an entry and a stop you can act on without re-deriving them, you need a read grounded in live candle data rather than pixels.
  • The same standard every time. A product applies its process identically on your calm days and your tilted ones. A prompt only works when you remember to use it properly, which is exactly not the moment you need it.
  • Permission to refuse. A tool with no engagement incentive can return an explicit wait. A chat model tuned for helpfulness usually finds something encouraging to say.
  • A record you can audit. Reads stored with timestamps, scored against what actually happened, are the raw material for improving. Scrolling back through a chat thread is not that.

FAQ

Is there a free AI that analyses trading charts?

Yes. General chat models with vision read chart screenshots at no cost and describe structure well. Several purpose-built tools offer a limited free allowance. The differences are grounding in live data, whether the tool may refuse a setup, and whether results are published.

Can I analyse a chart with AI without signing up?

Some tools allow it. Ours runs a demo analysis with no account at /try, then a 3-day free trial after signup with no card. Most competing free tiers require an account first, and some require a card.

What is the catch with free AI chart analysis?

Usually one of three: a card required before the trial, a free tier that never refuses a setup because it exists to sell an upgrade, or invented price levels because nothing grounds the model in live candles.

Is free AI chart analysis accurate?

Good at structure, bad at numbers. A vision model reading an image commonly invents levels that are plausible and wrong by enough to matter. Verify every level on your own chart before acting.

When is it worth paying?

When you need the same standard applied every time without writing the prompt, live data behind the read, a tool permitted to say no trade, and a published track record. Otherwise, free is enough.
