How it works, concretely

  1. Open the chart in TradingView. Draw your setup or leave it bare.
  2. Screenshot the chart. On desktop TradingView, Ctrl+Alt+S (Cmd+Alt+S on Mac) copies the chart image straight to your clipboard.
  3. Paste it into Tickrify. Ctrl+V works directly, no upload dialog.
  4. Read what comes back: bias, entry, stop, targets, the confluence it found, the risk, the scenarios it considers, and what would prove the read wrong.

The output is not a verdict you follow. It is a structured second read against which you compare your own. When it agrees with you, that is a small amount of extra confidence. When it disagrees, that is the useful case, and it will tell you what specifically it disagrees about.

How to frame a screenshot the model can actually read

This is the single biggest lever on output quality and almost nobody mentions it. A model reading a chart is doing optical work, and you can make that work easy or hard.

  • Keep the price axis visible. Without it the model has no numbers to anchor to, only shapes. If you have the right axis hidden for a clean look, turn it back on before you capture.
  • Keep the symbol and timeframe in frame. Tickrify reads both off the image. The timeframe in particular matters: if it cannot be read, the live candle block is skipped entirely, because computing ATR and swing structure on the wrong interval is worse than computing nothing.
  • Show enough history. Roughly 100 to 200 candles. A chart zoomed into the last twelve bars gives the model no structure to read, and structure is most of what it is looking for.
  • Do not stack six indicators. Overlays cover the candles. Price action plus one or two things you actually use reads far better than a chart buried under studies.
  • Capture at full width. A narrow crop compresses the candles into noise. The wider the capture, the more legible the wicks.
  • Use the chart you are actually trading. Not a cleaned-up version. If your entry logic depends on a trendline you drew, leave the trendline on.

What happens after it reads your screenshot

This is where Tickrify separates from pasting a chart into a general chatbot. Once the instrument and timeframe are read off your image, the backend goes and gets the real data for them, and puts that data into the prompt as text.

  • Up to 120 recent candles on your timeframe, from OKX with Binance as fallback for crypto, or Twelve Data for gold, silver and forex.
  • ATR14, so distances are expressed in a unit that means the same thing on BTC and on EURUSD.
  • Swing highs and lows, and where current price sits inside the visible range as a percentage.
  • RSI(14) Wilder, with an explicit instruction that an extreme reading is not a reversal trigger on its own.
  • Volume against its 20-period average, so a spike is labelled as a spike rather than guessed at.
  • A volume-by-price map: point of control, value area, and the low-volume gaps price tends to move through quickly.
  • An event map: the last impulse leg and its origin, recent structure breaks, accumulation bases and which way they resolved, and failed breakouts of the spring and upthrust kind.

Counting and locating things is what vision models do worst. Turning those steps into deterministic arithmetic before the model sees them removes an entire category of confident error.

The stale screenshot guard

If you upload a TradingView capture you saved three days ago, injecting the live price next to it would be actively harmful: the model would build entry, stop and target from numbers that contradict the chart in front of it, and the result would look authoritative and be nonsense.

So the backend reads the last visible close off your screenshot and compares it to the live close. More than three ATRs apart and the live data is withheld. The model is told it is looking at a historical chart, that every level must come from the image, and that it must say so in the output. You get an honest weaker read instead of a confident wrong one.

Which TradingView instruments get live data

Not all of them, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Crypto pairs, gold, silver and forex majors get verified candles. Index CFDs and single stocks do not, and on those the analysis works from your screenshot alone. The full coverage table is here.

Why there is no Pine Script indicator

It gets asked often enough to answer plainly. Pine runs inside your chart and cannot call an external model, so an indicator version would have to be a thin shell that ships your chart somewhere else anyway. It would add an install step, a permissions conversation and a maintenance surface, and it would deliver the same analysis you can get by pressing Ctrl+Alt+S and Ctrl+V. When the honest version of a feature is worse than the thing it replaces, we would rather not ship it.

What it will not do

  • It will not place, modify or close a trade. There is no broker connection and there will not be one.
  • It will not scan or screen. You bring the chart.
  • It will not read the news. No headlines, no sentiment, no order flow.
  • It will not always give you a trade. A large share of reads come back as "no trade", and that abstention is the feature. If you want something that always finds a setup, plenty of tools will oblige you.

Questions people actually ask

Does Tickrify connect to my TradingView account?

No. There is no integration, no API key and no login. You paste an image. That is the entire surface.

Do I need a paid TradingView plan?

No. A free TradingView account produces screenshots that work fine. The features that help most, a visible price axis and a legible timeframe, are on the free tier.

How is this different from TradingView's own AI assistant?

Two things. It is willing to come back with "no trade", and its results are published with sample sizes and confidence intervals on our accuracy page. If the in-chart copilot already does what you need, you may not need us.

Can it read my drawings and annotations?

It reads what is visible in the image, drawings included. Leaving your trendlines and zones on generally helps, because the read is then about the setup you are actually considering.

Does it work with the TradingView mobile app?

Yes. A phone screenshot works as long as the price axis and the timeframe are in frame. Landscape captures more structure than portrait.

Is Tickrify a TradingView replacement?

No, and it is not trying to be. No charting, no alerts, no screeners, no broker integration. If you had to pick one, pick the charting platform.
