What Tickrify can actually read

Updated August 2026

Every AI chart tool says it "analyzes any chart". Most of them mean the model looks at your image and guesses. Tickrify does something narrower and more checkable: for eligible quick analyses, it attempts to identify the instrument and timeframe, fetch candles and verify that the feed agrees with your screenshot. When that check fails, the read uses the image alone and reports the missing data.

That only works where a real feed exists. On this page we tell you exactly where it does and where it does not, because a tool that quietly guesses on the instruments it cannot verify is worse than one that admits the gap.

Instrument coverage, in full

"Live candles" below means the same thing every time: the backend pulls up to 120 recent candles on your chart's timeframe, computes ATR14, swing highs and lows, RSI(14) Wilder, and an event map of the last impulse leg, recent structure breaks and failed breakouts. Those numbers go into the prompt as text. The model reads arithmetic instead of pixels.

Two of those measures depend on volume, which not every market has. On crypto, where volume is real and centralized, the analysis also gets volume against its 20-period average and a volume-by-price map with the point of control and value area. On spot forex and metals there is no centralized volume, so those two blocks are left out entirely rather than estimated. That is a deliberate choice in the code, not an oversight: an invented point of control is worse than no point of control.

The one guard worth knowing about

Screenshots go stale. People save a chart, come back two days later and upload it. If Tickrify blindly injected "the current price is X" next to a chart drawn at a different price, the model would build an entry, a stop and a target around numbers that contradict the image, and the output would look confident and be wrong.

So before injecting anything, the backend compares the last visible close it read off your chart against the live close. If those two are more than three ATRs apart, the live data is withheld and the model is told in plain language that it is looking at a historical chart, that every level must come from the image, and that it must say so in the output. You get a slightly weaker analysis instead of a confidently wrong one.

What we do not claim

  • No breaking news, no sentiment, no order flow. The model does not read headlines. If a story broke ten minutes ago, it has no idea.
  • No execution. Tickrify cannot place, modify or close a trade, and will not. A tool that both advises and executes has an incentive problem we would rather not have.
  • No promise that it is right. We publish the measured hit rate, the sample size and the 95% confidence interval on our accuracy page, including the parts that look bad. As far as we can tell, no competitor publishes anything comparable.
  • It abstains often. A large share of reads come back as "no trade". That is the product working, not failing.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need to connect my broker or exchange?

No. Nothing connects. You upload an image, and the backend uses an AI provider for analysis and may call public market data feeds for an eligible quick read.

What happens if it cannot identify my instrument?

It falls back to reading the screenshot alone and returns a structured read without live candles. Check any proposed price level against your chart before trading.

Which timeframes work best?

Anything with a readable price axis and a legible timeframe label. If the timeframe cannot be read, the candle block is skipped on purpose, because statistics computed on the wrong interval hurt more than they help.

Is this a signal service?

No. It reads the chart cold and tells you what it sees, including when the answer is to do nothing. Arrive with a thesis and it works as a second opinion. Arrive with none and it is the first one. The decision stays yours. If you want somebody to tell you what to buy, this is the wrong product.
