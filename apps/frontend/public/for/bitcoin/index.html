What happens when you upload a Bitcoin chart

You paste a screenshot. Before the model writes a word about price, the backend reads the symbol and timeframe off the image and fetches the real data for them.

  • Up to 120 recent candles on your timeframe, from OKX as the primary source with Binance as fallback.
  • ATR14, so every distance in the output is expressed in a volatility unit rather than in raw dollars. A 400 dollar stop means one thing on a quiet day and something else entirely on a violent one.
  • Swing highs and lows, and where price currently sits inside the visible range as a percentage.
  • RSI(14) Wilder, delivered with an explicit instruction that an extreme reading is not on its own a reversal trigger. Crypto stays overbought for weeks.
  • Volume against its 20-period average, so a genuine spike gets labelled as one instead of eyeballed.
  • A volume-by-price map: point of control, value area, and the low-volume gaps price tends to travel through fast.
  • An event map: the last impulse leg and where it started, recent structure breaks, accumulation bases and which side they resolved to, and failed breakouts of the spring and upthrust kind.

Counting candles, locating a swing and reading a level off an axis are the three things vision models are worst at. Doing them in deterministic arithmetic before the model ever sees the numbers removes a whole family of confident errors.

Why 24/7 changes the read

Crypto never closes, and that has consequences most chart tools ignore.

  • Weekend liquidity is thinner. The same structure break on a Sunday afternoon means less than it would on a Tuesday, because it took less size to produce it. Volume against the 20-period average is exactly the number that exposes this, and it goes into every read.
  • There are no gaps to lean on. Techniques built around session opens and overnight gaps do not transfer. The model works from range, volume distribution and structure instead.
  • Your screenshot ages fast. Which is why the stale chart guard matters more here than anywhere else.

The stale screenshot guard

Crypto moves while you are deciding. If you upload a chart you saved two hours ago and the backend blindly injected the current price beside it, the model would build an entry, a stop and a target on numbers that contradict the image in front of it, and the result would read as confident and be wrong.

So the last visible close is read off your screenshot and compared to the live close. If they are more than three ATRs apart, the live data is withheld and the model is told plainly that it is looking at a historical chart, that every level must come from the image, and that the output must say so. A weaker honest read beats a confident wrong one.

What it does not know

  • No news, no announcements, no unlocks. The model does not read headlines. A listing, a hack, an exchange outage or a token unlock happened as far as it is concerned in a universe it cannot see.
  • No on-chain data. No flows, no whale wallets, no funding rates, no open interest. It reads price, volume and structure.
  • No social sentiment. Whatever is happening on Crypto Twitter right now is invisible to it.
  • No execution. It cannot place, modify or close a trade, and there is no exchange connection to make that possible.

If your edge in crypto comes from being first to information, this tool adds nothing. If it comes from not taking the marginal setup, that is exactly the gap it is built for.

It will often tell you not to trade

A large share of reads come back as "no trade". In a range with two-way rotation and price sitting in the middle, there is no directional pressure to build a plan from, and the honest output is to say so rather than manufacture a setup. Most tools in this category will not do that, because a tool that returns nothing feels broken to a new user.

Questions people actually ask

Does it connect to my exchange?

No. There is no API key, no read-only key and no login. You upload an image, and the only outbound calls are to public market data endpoints.

Does it work for altcoins?

For the 40 bases listed above, with live candles. For anything else it reads your screenshot and returns a structured analysis without a verified candle block, and it says so rather than inventing a price.

Does it work for perpetuals and futures charts?

Yes. A perp chart resolves to the same underlying base, so BTCUSDT.P and BTCUSDT get the same candle feed. It does not read funding or open interest.

Which timeframes work best?

Any timeframe legible on the chart. If the timeframe label cannot be read, the candle block is skipped deliberately, because ATR and swing structure computed on the wrong interval mislead more than they help.

Can it tell me when to buy Bitcoin?

No, and be wary of anything that says it can. It reads the chart cold and gives you a read of its own, including the case against it and what would prove it wrong. The decision stays yours.
